# HAZARD : **RAIN**
**Rain does not damage an event directly** — it degrades the surfaces, materials, and pathways that the event depends on, and the resulting problems accumulate over time. The YOUROPE evidence explicitly notes that heavy rain is often underestimated because its effects develop gradually rather than as a single sudden failure.


The underlying mechanism has two parts:

1.**Ground and surface degradation** — soil, grass, and temporary surfaces lose stability and traction as they absorb water, with the rate and severity depending on soil type, drainage capacity, and site slope.

2.**Cumulative operational strain** — because the effect builds gradually, incidents are frequently caused not by the rainfall itself but by delayed recognition of its cumulative impact on ground conditions and access routes.

## **Immediate Physical Effects**

- Slippery, unstable, or waterlogged ground surfaces
- Reduced traction on walkways, ramps, and temporary flooring
- Pooling or localized flooding in low-lying or poorly-drained areas
- Reduced visibility during heavy downpours
- Degradation of unsealed or temporary electrical/structural connections exposed to moisture

## **What It Affects**

| Affected Party | Specific Impact |
|---|---|
| **Attendees** | Slips and falls, reduced mobility, discomfort-driven congestion at shelter points, potential medical incidents from crowd behaviour in wet conditions |
| **Staff** | Increased physical strain managing wet-ground incidents, reduced ability to move quickly across site, extended shift demands during prolonged rain |
| **Access Routes** | Slippery surfaces and queuing at ingress; congestion, falls, and vehicle movement difficulties at egress; degraded access roads for medium/long events |
| **Venue** | Site damage to ground surfaces and temporary infrastructure; difficulties with equipment recovery during breakdown; potential damage to unsealed structures |

# HAZARD : **EXTREME HEAT**

**Heat risk is a physiological accumulation hazard, not an environmental damage hazard** — unlike rain, which degrades the site, heat directly degrades the human body's ability to regulate temperature over time. The combination of temperature, humidity, radiant/solar load, and the body's ability to cool itself (via shade, wind, water, and rest) drives the actual risk, not air temperature alone.

The mechanism compounds with **exposure duration and crowd density**: a high-density crowd in direct sun with limited shade and poor ventilation reaches dangerous thermal load far faster than the same temperature would suggest in isolation, which is why "feels like" temperature is a better indicator than raw air temperature for this hazard.

## **Immediate Physical Effects**

- Elevated body temperature and dehydration risk among attendees and staff
- Reduced physical performance and judgment, particularly during physically demanding activity
- Heat-related illness symptoms (cramps, exhaustion, heat stroke) increasing in frequency and severity with prolonged exposure
- Increased strain on cooling infrastructure (water points, shaded areas) as demand rises
- Potential degradation of heat-sensitive equipment or materials in direct, prolonged sun exposure

## **What It Affects**

| Affected Party | Specific Impact |
|---|---|
| **Attendees** | Heat exhaustion, heat stroke, dehydration, fainting — risk compounds with crowd density and limited shade/water access |
| **Staff** | Same physiological risks as attendees, compounded by extended exposure duration (full shifts) and physical exertion required by their roles |
| **Access Routes** | Queuing areas with limited shade become high-exposure zones; extended wait times at ingress increase cumulative heat exposure before the event even begins |
| **Venue** | Increased load on cooling/water infrastructure; potential need for additional shaded medical stations; heat-vulnerable equipment or temporary structures may require monitoring |

# HAZARD : **THUNDERSTORM (LIGHTNING, WIND, HEAVY RAIN)**

**Thunderstorms are a compound, fast-onset hazard rather than a single-variable one** — the risk mechanism is the simultaneous combination of lightning, sudden strong gusts, and heavy rain arriving with limited lead time, which is fundamentally different from the gradual-accumulation mechanisms of plain rain or heat.

Because a thunderstorm can escalate from negligible to dangerous within minutes, the core risk mechanism is **decision-time compression**: the hazard leaves little room for adaptive response, so risk is managed through predefined thresholds and decision authority rather than real-time judgment. Thunderstorm-driven hazards (lightning, downbursts, flash floods) fall into the "immediate–short term" category, capable of forcing instant stage clearance or full site evacuation.

## **Immediate Physical Effects**

- Direct lightning strike risk to exposed individuals and ungrounded structures
- Sudden strong gusts capable of destabilizing temporary structures with little warning
- Rapid-onset heavy rain and localized flash flooding
- Sudden drops in visibility
- Potential simultaneous power loss or equipment failure from lightning-related electrical disruption

## **What It Affects**

| Affected Party | Specific Impact |
|---|---|
| **Attendees** | Direct lightning strike risk, injury from sudden structural failure or panic-driven crowd movement, risk of being caught in flash flooding during evacuation |
| **Staff** | Same direct risks as attendees, plus responsibility for executing time-critical evacuation/show-stop decisions under pressure |
| **Access Routes** | Egress routes may become simultaneously congested (mass evacuation) and physically degraded (flooding, debris) at the same time |
| **Venue** | Risk of structural damage to stages/tents from sudden gusts, power loss from lightning strikes, and potential need for full-site evacuation rather than localized response |

# HAZARD : **HIGH WIND**

**Wind risk is a structural-integrity hazard that progressively worsens rather than acting as a single-threshold event.** Risk is driven by **gusts and turbulence, not sustained average wind**, and by **secondary effects** such as debris and the progressive loosening of anchors over time — meaning a structure that withstands an initial gust may still fail later in the same event as anchoring points weaken cumulatively.

Risk exposure is also **highly site- and phase-dependent**: the same wind speed poses different risk levels depending on local topography (open fields vs. sheltered sites) and operational phase (structures are most vulnerable during build-up, before full anchoring is complete).

## **Immediate Physical Effects**

- Structural instability or collapse of stages, tents, screens, and signage
- Airborne debris (loose equipment, temporary fixtures, signage)
- Progressive loosening of anchor points and structural connections over time
- Disruption to suspended equipment (lighting rigs, speaker arrays)
- Turbulence-driven unpredictability, making risk harder to anticipate than sustained wind alone

## **What It Affects**

| Affected Party | Specific Impact |
|---|---|
| **Attendees** | Risk of injury from structural collapse or flying debris, particularly in crowded areas near stages or temporary structures |
| **Staff** | Risk during build-up/breakdown phases when structures are incomplete or being dismantled; responsibility for securing structures and restricting access to at-risk areas |
| **Access Routes** | Potential blockage from fallen debris or structures; areas near tall or temporary structures may require dynamic access restrictions as wind conditions change |
| **Venue** | Direct structural risk to stages, tents, signage, and suspended equipment; risk compounds over the event's duration as anchoring degrades, not just at peak gust moments |
